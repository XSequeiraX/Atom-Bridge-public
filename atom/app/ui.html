PREFLIGHT CHECK One answer to "can I shoot?". Green lines are
               fine, red ones are not. A pending fault is listed but does not block.


            
CLOCK

            
Three rows, three jobs. The top row picks which clock
               runs the show and is the only row that carries state. The middle row
               fetches from a source. The bottom row forgets one. A
               column is a source, top to bottom: LTC (the stored jam), NTP, IPHONE,
               and BM. BM has no fetch and no clear — it is live or it is not.
               Nothing is picked at boot — every start comes up with no clock
               source at all, on purpose, so a stale choice from a previous session
               can never run quietly. Pick one before rolling.

            
Reading the top row. Filled green means that source has a value
               right now. White means it has none. A green outline on top of the fill
               means that is the one driving the system — only ever one. An amber,
               blinking outline means it is driving but its fetch is more than six
               hours old. A red outline means the source you picked has died
               and, since nothing falls back automatically, nothing is setting the
               cameras until you fetch again or pick another.

            
Nothing switches by itself. Picking a source you do not have is
               refused with a message telling you what to press, rather than accepted
               in silence. And connecting the BMPCC no longer steals the reference: it
               simply becomes available, and you choose it if you want it. Two
               timecodes can silently disagree, so which one governs is your call.

            
NTP and IPHONE are the same physical clock. The system has one
               clock; those two differ only in where its time last came from. That is
               why picking NTP without ever having pressed GET NTP counts as a source
               you do not have, instead of quietly meaning "use whatever time is in
               there".

            
GET LTC Read the generator through the USB audio input and store
               the offset. After this you can take the TC-1 away and the system keeps the
               time. Also picks up the date from the LTC user bits. It refuses a
               reading whose date disagrees with the system clock, not just one whose time
               does: the Deity app only updates the date when you confirm it there, and
               a stale date carries the right time of day, so it would otherwise sail
               through and anchor the whole session to the wrong day. Confirm the date
               in the app, or force it if you know better.

            
BT (next to GET LTC) Same as GET LTC, but over Bluetooth, with no
               cable: it listens to the TC-1s for about 15 seconds. Open the Sidus app
               first, with the timecodes running — the TC-1s only send their time
               while it is open. It refuses if they disagree with each other by more than
               one frame (sync them again in the app).

            
LTC counter blinking amber The stored reading is more than six
               hours old. It still works, but the system clock has been drifting
               since it was taken, so the further you get from it the less it is worth.
               Connect the generator and press GET LTC; the counter goes back to steady
               green. This is why the warning lives on the counter and not inside this
               panel: you will not have it open while shooting.

            
BOTH TIMECODES AT ONCE When the BMPCC is connected its timecode
               governs and turns green, but the stored LTC stays visible above it,
               dimmed. Next to the camera's counter you get the difference between the
               two, as +Ns vs LTC. This exists because they can silently
               disagree: connecting the camera used to swap the reference without
               showing you that the two were seconds apart.

            
CLEAR LTC / CLEAR NTP / CLEAR IPHONE TIME Forget a source, so
               it goes back to white. None of them changes the system clock — it
               has only one and it keeps whatever time it has; what you are clearing is
               the claim about where that time came from. Useful when you have driven
               somewhere else and do not want this morning's provenance still counting
               as current.

            
SET SYSTEM CLOCK FROM LTC Push the generator's date and time into the
               system clock. Only useful on location with no internet; refuses when NTP is
               synced and already agrees.

            
GET NTP Ask for an immediate NTP sync. Needs an uplink, and
               refuses without one rather than leaving the clock marked untrusted.
               The system never syncs on its own any more — this button is the only way
               it goes and gets the time from the network, and it switches the
               background sync daemon off again the moment it is done.

            
SYNC ALL CAM TIME AND DATE Write the active source's time and date
               into every connected camera. Once per session, before you roll.


            
TAKE DATA Opens on every device as soon as the last camera stops:
               session name, scene, reel, next shot, good / no-good and a note, all
               optional. SAVE or SKIP closes it everywhere. Until then no new
               recording starts.


            
GOOD / NO-GOOD / MARK Verdicts at any moment, written to the shoot
               log with the timecode. MARK is the unclassified one, for
               flagging something that happened between takes. Each shows the
               timecode it recorded so you can see it worked.


            
UPLINK ON 2.4 GHz (amber, main page) The uplink wifi is on the same
               band as Bluetooth and the camera access point. Measured on this rig: with the uplink on 2.4 GHz the
               camera link died every 16 to 45 seconds; moved to 5 GHz it ran for
               minutes untouched. If it says PS_Setup AP ON 2.4 GHz it is worse
               still — the setup access point then transmits on that band too.
               Switch networks, or take the access point down.


            
BACKUP LOGS TO SERVER Sends the shoot log and the event log to
               your own machine over SFTP, plus every archived copy still on the card.
               The current files go with a timestamp in the remote name and the archives
               already carry one, so nothing ever overwrites anything on the
               server. It runs in the background — the result lands in the EVENT LOG.
               Archiving on the card protects you from a mistake; this protects you from
               the card dying.

            
SFTP SERVER SETTINGS Host (a Tailscale name works), port, username,
               password and remote folder. The password is stored on the bridge in a file
               only root can read, and is never sent back to this page — leave the
               field blank to keep the one already saved.

            
SHOOT LOG / EVENT LOG (main page buttons) Open their own pages
               (/shootlog, /eventlog) — full-size tables instead
               of a cramped inline box.

            
NEW (on the shoot log / event log page) Archives the current file
               with the date in its name and starts an empty one. Nothing is
               deleted. On the shoot log, take numbering restarts at 1, and it
               refuses mid-take — a start in one file and a stop in another leaves the
               take with no duration in either.

            
Editing a shoot log row Click a cell (action, take, or note) on the
               /shootlog page to edit it in place — no prompts. It saves when
               you tab or click away, or press Enter; Escape cancels. The delete button
               sits in the first column, with the time/source/event right next to it, so
               you see what you are about to remove. Both rewrite the CSV in place,
               because that file is read in post by someone who was not here — a wrong
               entry sitting next to the right one is worse than no entry. What you
               changed is written to the event log for this session.


            
CAMERAS AND ALARMS

            
WARN ON UNCOMMANDED STOP Raise the alarm if a camera stops on its
               own. Leave it off if you press REC on the bodies themselves, or it will
               cry wolf every time.

            
TEST ALARM (FAILED RECORDING) Fires a fake fault so you can see
               what a real one looks like. Faults stay until you dismiss them.

            
TEST WARNING (BATTERY) Same, for a warning. Warnings clear
               themselves after a minute; faults do not.

            
RESET CANON CAMS Drags a Canon out of a stuck state: still
               recording, or trapped in movie mode, after a reply got lost. Sends stop,
               then retries leaving movie mode until the body accepts.


            
CAMERA CARDS

            
Closed by default. Each camera card shows its name, state, link and
               first line of data, plus any warning. Tap the state line or the arrow to
               open it; open cards stay open on this device. Tap a name with ✎ to
               rename it. ALIAS is the short name that goes in the shoot log.

            
☰ (top left) Opens this panel: recovery actions, clock, logs
               and HELP. The number is the Atom's RAM in use.

            
CAMS 5/7, GW 2/3 Cameras connected out of those in the rig, and gateways
               answering out of those switched on. Red: an armed camera is offline, or a
               gateway does not answer. Tap to jump to it. Each camera connects and
               disconnects from its own card (CONNECT / DISCONNECT).


            
ZOOM F6

            
NOT CONNECTED On the F6: MENU > FRC-8, connect with the USB cable
               unplugged, then plug it in. Until then REC skips the F6 and only JAM TC and
               CARD are shown.

            
SCENE NAME / SCENE + Only with the F6 Scene Name Mode on User Name
               (MENU > REC > Metadata > Scene Name > Mode).


            
NETWORK

            
WIFI LED Turns the dongle's blinking light off. Useful when the
               rig is in shot.

            
WIFI STATUS The two radios separately: the uplink, with signal
               quality and address, and the camera access point with who is joined to
               it. Cameras are named; anything else counts as "other". Also flags the
               helper services if any of them is down.

            
TAILSCALE STATUS Whether the remote-access tunnel is up, this
               machine's address on it, and how many of your devices are reachable.
               Worth checking before you walk away from the rig: with the tunnel
               down, the only way in is the camera access point.

            
CHOOSE WIFI NETWORK Lists the networks already saved on the system
               with their signal and band, strongest first, and switches the uplink to
               the one you pick. 2.4 GHz networks are marked amber: that band is
               shared with Bluetooth, and an uplink there measurably costs you camera
               link quality. Inside it, REFRESH KNOWN NETWORKS re-checks which of them
               are in range — it is not automatic, because scanning takes over the
               radio and that on its own has dropped live links.

            
RESTART CAMERA WIFI Restarts the camera access point. The
               Canon bodies drop and rejoin.


            
A NETWORK NEVER SEEN BEFORE Is joined on the bridge itself: CHOOSE
               WIFI NETWORK on its screen, or the start-up menu. From a phone you can
               only switch between networks already saved.

            
RESTART BLUETOOTH Restarts the Bluetooth stack. The BMPCC drops
               for about fifteen seconds.

            
RESTART NETWORKMANAGER The big hammer for wedged networking. Every
               wifi drops briefly. Bluetooth is unaffected.


            
LAST RESORT

            
RESTART BRIDGE Restarts this software only. Refuses while
               recording.

            
REBOOT SYSTEM Reboots the bridge computer. About a minute unavailable.

            
SHUT DOWN SYSTEM Only comes back with physical access.
